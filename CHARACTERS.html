Characters

Everyone in the village has their own worries. Nobody exists to teach the player a lesson.

How they look

Round, plush animals (src/ui/plush.ts): You are a red panda, Mara a deer, Tobin an otter, Wren a goat, Oda a badger, Pip and Juno mice (Juno has a bow), Nell a sheep, Ash a bear. Each has their own face (eyes, brows, mouth, head shape) that fits who they are, and keeps their outfit colors and props (Mara's clipboard and scarf, Tobin's cap and apron, Wren's baker's hat, Nell's shawl, cane and flower, Ash's glasses).

Feelings. Eleven, each changing eyes, brows, mouth and cheeks, adding a small sign by the head, and moving the body: calm (breathing), happy (sparkles, a bounce), laughing (a wobble), warm, surprised ("!", a hop), worried (a sweat drop, a sway), sad (a tear, a sink), upset (a puff of steam, a stomp), scared (a sweat drop, a shiver), embarrassed (blush lines, eyes down, shrinking smaller), tired ("zzz", a droop). While someone speaks, their mouth opens and closes with the words, in the village and in their portrait; everyone blinks.

Rules for writing dialogue

  • No lectures: no character explains a skill or tells the player what they should have done.
  • Costs come from people's own lives: Mara thinks she upset you; Tobin thinks his laugh drove you

off. Nobody blames or shames the player.

  • Characters notice different things: most of them missed the fall entirely. That is what the

facts are made of.

  • Speaking lines are written in Ink as id: text or id/feeling: text. Feelings:

neutral, happy, laughing, warm, surprised, worried, sad, upset, scared, embarrassed, tired (the older smile and laugh still work).

The square

Mara (mara): runs the festival. Thirties, scarf, clipboard energy. Kind, organized, a worrier. When things go quiet she assumes she caused it, which is the player's pattern seen from the outside. Her grandmother started the festival, and her grandmother's paper lantern is the oldest thing Mara owns. Says "okay, okay" when stressed.

Tobin (tobin): the lantern-maker's apprentice, early twenties, cap and apron full of wire. Laughs when nervous or surprised, never to be cruel. He tripped on the same loose stone this morning, broke one of his own lanterns, and has been joking about it all day so nobody else can first. The one laugh the player hears is his.

Wren and Oda (wren, oda): the bakers, married, fifties. Wren (tall, baker's hat) frets that the bread won't be ready by sundown. Oda (short, flour on everything) says it always is. They bicker fondly and notice almost nothing outside their oven.

Pip and Juno (pip, juno): siblings, about seven and nine. Pip is delighted by everything; Juno counts things and corrects people kindly. They think the red lantern rolling across the square was the best thing that happened all day.

Your lane

Nell (nell): the neighbor on your lane, in her eighties, shawl and cane, always on her step. Has watched sixty-one festivals. Dry humor. Doesn't give advice; tells a story instead, usually one where she was the one who got it wrong. Notices when the lane goes dark and isn't worried by it.

Ash (ash): your friend, runs the tea cart, glasses and a long scarf. Steady and doesn't push. If you hide, Ash comes up the hill, calls into the hollow once, and leaves tea on the rock. Ash treats rest as allowed and is still glad when you come back.

Conversations

Talking to someone opens a menu of topics you can come back to (talk_* knots at the end of story/festival.ink). Topics change with the part of the story (phase: afternoon, finding out what happened, festival, the morning after, after the storm) and open up as you learn things. The conversations the story depends on (finding out what happened, the morning errands, telling someone you're resting) are topics too. A bright, bobbing speech bubble means someone you haven't talked to yet in this part of the story; a faint one means you have, and can again.

Each villager's story runs quietly through all three chapters. None of them is a lesson; they mirror the player's situation from the outside.

Full backstories

This is the background the dialogue is written from. Most of it never appears on screen; the parts that do are marked On screen. Write new lines so they could be true of the person below, and never use a backstory to explain a skill or to make the player feel they caused someone's trouble.

You (red panda)

Kept loose on purpose, so any player can step in: no family details, no gender, no job.

  • History: moved to Lantern Lane at eight, the new one who knew nobody. Found the hollow

in the rocks the first month and went there whenever something went wrong. At ten, Ash found you there, sat outside without saying much, and waited until the tea went cold. You chipped Ash's gran's cup at twelve and cried for an hour.

  • The years away: you left for some years and came back last spring. This is your first

festival back. Carrying the lanterns is the first job anyone has given you since; it means you belong here again, which is why dropping them lands so hard.

  • What people know: Nell has always known where you went (On screen, afternoon: "I

always knew where you'd gone"). Ash knows about the hollow and comes to it (On screen, if you hid). Mara asked you because you came back (On screen, afternoon).

Mara (deer)

  • Family: Rosa's granddaughter. Spent summers in Rosa's house on Lantern Lane as a child

and moved in at twenty when Rosa needed help. Her mother lives in the city and visits at New Year. Mara still lives in Rosa's house; the supper invitations are for there.

  • The festival: took it over at twenty-nine, when Rosa died. Rosa never used a list; Mara

has eleven. The lists are how she keeps the fear of doing it wrong somewhere she can see it.

  • What she fears: being the one who lets the festival fade. Underneath that: that people

were only ever kind to her because of Rosa. That's why her first guess, when anything goes quiet, is that she caused it.

  • What she wants: one festival where nothing goes wrong. She finds out it never happened,

even for Rosa.

  • The box: a shoebox in the attic marked FESTIVAL, untouched for five years. Inside, sixty

years of pencil notes, almost all of them mistakes ("Year three. Forgot the candles. Used turnips."). At the bottom, the oldest note: the first lantern was for Nell, and Rosa painted her door. On screen, after the storm, if you heard how the festival started.

  • Habits: says "okay, okay" when stressed; walks the same loop of the square when

thinking; keeps Rosa's lantern on the top shelf, away from everything.

Tobin (otter)

  • Family: from a fishing town on the coast. His dad, Fen, jokes through everything,

including the years the boats came back empty. Tobin loves him, and is slowly working out which jokes help and which ones hide things. His mum is quiet and sends knitted socks.

  • The workshop: came at sixteen to apprentice with Hesketh, who retired to the coast two

years ago and writes long letters about glue. Tobin made eleven of this year's lanterns; the red one is the first he designed himself, and it's a little crooked (On screen, main story). Hesketh has offered him the workshop for good. He hasn't answered in three weeks, because an apprentice is allowed to get it wrong and an owner isn't. On screen: he tells you at the festival, and says yes after the storm.

  • What he fears: being laughed at for really trying. So he laughs first.
  • Habits: twists wire while he talks; counts things for Juno so she doesn't have to;

says "Ha" after anything that matters.

Wren (goat) and Oda (badger)

  • History: the bakery was Oda's aunt Bet's. Wren came for one summer, twenty-five years

ago, as seasonal help. She burned the first batch, Oda laughed, and Wren stayed. Married twenty years.

  • Wren: tall, quick, a worrier about bread and nothing else out loud. Her back has hurt

since last spring. She's afraid that slowing down means getting old, and that a slow bakery is a bakery people stop coming to.

  • Oda: short, floury, unbothered. For a year she has been getting up at three instead of

four to do Wren's share of the kneading before Wren comes down, and has never said so, because Wren would never have agreed. On screen: you notice she's tired in chapter two, and Wren finds out after the storm. They decide to hire help, out loud.

  • Pip and Juno: the bakers give them breakfast most mornings and mind them during the

festival.

Pip and Juno (mice)

  • Family: their mum is a nurse at the infirmary in Haddon, the next town, and works

nights, so she sleeps in the day (On screen, chapter two). Nobody else in their family is mentioned; don't add anyone.

  • Juno (nine): started counting things when their mum first went on nights, counting the

days to Saturday. Now she counts whenever it's loud: 412 cobblestones, 73 flags. She corrects people kindly and is quietly in charge of Pip.

  • Pip (seven): has decided this is the year she's big enough to stay up all night. Named

Tobin's red lantern Strawberry ("It's a strawberry in its heart"). The storm is her first time being really scared in front of everyone.

Nell (sheep)

  • History: eighty-four. Grew up across the lane from Rosa; they were girls together.

Married Wil at twenty-one; both worked at the mill. Wil died of a fever the autumn she was twenty-three, and in November the mill shut. That winter she couldn't face anybody and kept the curtains closed. She watched one light through them all winter: Rosa's lantern on Rosa's step. She thought Rosa had just taken to sitting out.

  • The door: in April she opened the door and it was yellow. Nobody owned up. She has

told people it was losing the mill; it was never the mill. On screen: the door story in the afternoon; if you open Rosa's box with Mara after the storm, you can tell Nell, and she says this herself.

  • Who she is now: has watched every festival from her step, without knowing they began

for her. Dry, unhurried, notices everything. Doesn't give advice; tells stories where she got it wrong. After hearing the truth, she plans to take Mara something yellow.

  • Habits: a flower in her shawl, always; counts festivals instead of birthdays.

Ash (bear)

  • Family: their parents run a tea company in the city with forty shops, and think the cart

is a phase (six years so far). Ash spent their school years in the village with their gran, Mae, who ran the tea cart and taught you both to drink tea (On screen, festival).

  • You: best friends since you were ten, when Ash found you in the hollow and sat outside.

The chipped cup is the one you broke at twelve; Ash said it was their favorite so you'd stop crying, and then it was (On screen, afternoon). That is why, if you hide, Ash knows exactly where to go and what to do (On screen, the morning after you hide).

  • The shop: wants to turn the old mill office, where Nell used to work, into a real tea

shop with chairs. Signs the lease in chapter two; opens the week after the festival. Afraid it will be "just okay".

  • Who they are: steady about other people's feelings and much less steady about their own;

only admits being scared if you ask. Pronouns: they/them.

Passing remarks

One-liners villagers say as you walk by (src/game/barks.ts), different in each part of the story. Under the shame haze, only people you've found out about make friendly remarks. Nobody chats during the storm or on the night you hid.
